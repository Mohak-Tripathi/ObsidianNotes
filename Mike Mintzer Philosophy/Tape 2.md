# Core Principles

## Principle 1

# Bodybuilding is Anaerobic

### Mentzer

Exercise science has two branches.

1. Aerobic
2. Anaerobic

Bodybuilding belongs completely to anaerobic exercise.

Aerobic:
- Low intensity
- Long duration
- Endurance

Anaerobic:
- High intensity
- Short duration
- Strength
- Muscle Size

Therefore bodybuilding should NEVER resemble endurance training.

Status

✅ Strongly Supported

Modern Evidence

Correct.

Resistance training is primarily anaerobic.

Application

Workout duration

Target:
60–75 minutes

Never train just because you still "feel fresh."

## Principle 2

# Intensity is the foundation

Mentzer's biggest claim.

He says intensity isn't merely important.

It is the foundation of exercise science.

Definition

Intensity

=

Percentage of possible momentary muscular effort.

Meaning

A set becomes progressively more productive as it approaches failure.

The final rep before failure is the most productive.

Status

🟡 Mostly Supported

Modern Evidence

Training close to failure produces excellent hypertrophy.

However,

Evidence does NOT prove

One set is always enough.

Application

Current Program

2 hard working sets

First:
0–1 RIR

Second:
Failure

# My Notes

Heavy Duty is NOT really a one-set system.

Heavy Duty is a recovery optimization system.

One set is simply Mentzer's proposed solution to minimizing fatigue.

The real philosophy is:

Maximum Adaptation
with
Minimum Necessary Training.


Overall => 

After reading it carefully, I think **Mentzer is often misunderstood**.

People think his philosophy is:

> **"One set to failure."**

I no longer think that's the core philosophy.

I think his actual philosophy is:

> **"The workout is a cost. Muscle growth is the return. Maximize return while minimizing cost."**

Everything else—one set, low volume, longer recovery—is a consequence of that philosophy.

I actually think that's a much deeper idea than "train less."

It also explains why you connected with it so much. As an engineer, you're naturally drawn to **optimization problems**: maximize output while minimizing wasted input. That's exactly how Mentzer frames bodybuilding in Tape 2.
